About

Why this exists.

I spent most of my career as a corporate executive, the kind of role built on decisions, deadlines, and being measured on results. I brought that same standard when I built Ripplepedia Studios.

I have a Bachelor of Science in Information Systems and an Executive MBA. That background is why I don't treat AI production as a shortcut. I treat it as a discipline, the same as any other production process, with a standard the work has to hit before it ships.

Here's what I believe, plainly: AI production isn't cheating and it isn't slop. It's the next chapter, the same shift film, lighting and production have been through before, and the people who spent years training in this industry have the advantage in it, not the disadvantage, if they treat it as a tool instead of a threat. A small or mid-sized company that could never afford a real production budget can now put out work that looks like it came from a studio, at a fraction of the cost and none of the wait.

AI is the next chapter in the evolution, not a shortcut around it.

Ripplepedia Studios exists to bring that standard to AI UGC, advertising, and short films, for teams who need output they can actually ship, not a proof of concept.

AI production changes fast enough that most people can't keep up with it, and I don't pretend otherwise. What I do instead is stay inside a small community of operators who make tracking every model release, workflow change, and new tool their actual job. That's not a nice-to-have. It's the difference between a studio still working the way it did six months ago and one that knows what's possible this week, and that gap is what ends up in the work I hand a client.

If you run an agency short on hands, a production house weighing a bid, or a brand trying to make a number look better by next quarter, I'd genuinely like to hear about it.
